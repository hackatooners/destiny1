# Quiet mind

Richard let the thought about the AI assistant pass, decided it wasn't worth the risk of
sounding like he was accusing anyone of anything, and just waited. Rosie came back on the line
with the tone people use right before bad news, soft and a little rehearsed. "I'm sorry,
Richard, they went with someone else for this one," she said, and then, almost as an
afterthought, "it came down to German, honestly, the role needed a speaker and it wasn't on
your profile."

Richard sat with that for a second, the disappointment briefly making room for something else.
"I do speak German," he said, "I just never thought to put it on the resume, it's not
something I use for work usually." He heard Rosie go quiet on her end, the kind of quiet that
meant she was deciding whether that changed anything or whether the decision was already
sealed somewhere upstream, in a system neither of them controlled anymore.

"Let me see what I can do," she said, and for the first time since the phone rang, Richard felt
like there was still a door open somewhere.
