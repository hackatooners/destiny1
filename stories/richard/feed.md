# Lost in the Feed

Richard put the phone down for a second, thumb catching the Instagram icon instead of the
green call button, and told himself it would just be a minute, just to clear his head before
he called Rosie. A video of a dog skateboarding turned into a video of someone plating pasta,
which turned into a stranger's vacation, which turned into forty-five minutes he never felt
pass.

When he finally looked up, the email was still open behind the app, that strange half-written
message from the assistant still sitting there: "do you want me to send the email now?" He
never noticed the timestamp on it, never guessed that somewhere on the other end an offer had
a clock on it, a small window that closed quietly while he was watching someone else's dog do
a kickflip.

By evening the silence started to feel less like patience and more like an answer. He never
found out he had needed to say yes within the hour, never found out the recruiter had been
waiting on a reply that was never going to come. He just kept scrolling, and the new job kept
being someone else's.

You lost the job.
