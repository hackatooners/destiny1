# AI Slop

When Rosie came back on the line, Richard didn't wait for her to speak first. "Before anything
else," he said, "I have to mention, the email I got, the body of it wasn't right, it looked
like some AI assistant had written half of it and forgot to finish, it said something like 'do
you want me to send the email now.'" He meant it as a favor, a heads up, proof he was paying
attention.

The line went quiet in a different way than before. Rosie's voice, when it came back, had lost
its warmth. "That's, huh, that's odd, let me look into that," she said, too carefully, and
Richard could hear something shift behind the words, could hear her deciding something about
him that had nothing to do with interview scores. He had just told a person that the tool she
used to do her job wrote her emails for her, and she was not going to forget it.

Two days later the rejection came through, clean and templated, no mention of AI or assistants
or slop, just "we've decided to move forward with another candidate." Richard reread the
sentence a few times, sure there was more to it than that, sure he had done nothing wrong
except notice something he wasn't supposed to say out loud.

Richard lost the job.
