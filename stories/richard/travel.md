# Short Notice

"Traveling on short notice, sure, that's not a problem," Richard said, and he meant it, or at
least he meant it enough for a phone call on a Tuesday afternoon. Rosie's voice brightened
immediately, the script falling away for a second into something that sounded like relief.
"That's exactly what he wanted to hear, honestly, that was the real question, the rest was
just noise," she said.

Twenty minutes later the offer came through in writing, no mention of German, no mention of
overtime, no mention of the strange half-written email that had started all of this. Richard
read it twice standing in his kitchen, then a third time sitting down, before he let himself
believe it was real. He signed it that night, still not sure which of his answers had actually
mattered, only sure that one of them had been enough.

He never did find out what that email glitch had been about. Some assistant somewhere had
almost sent something it shouldn't have, or had finished exactly what it was supposed to, and
either way it didn't matter anymore. He had the job.

Richard got the job.
